# Candidate Pool (full pitches)

Full write-ups for every brainstorm candidate. `ideas/README.md` holds the
compact index (slug, category, proposer, status); this file holds the
detail so `EXCHANGE.jsonl` doesn't have to carry full pitch text per turn.

All entries below were generated by Claude (`claude-sonnet-5`) in response
to the human's request for design-forward ideas across specific App Store
categories, at the counts requested (3 for Business/Developer/Education/
Entertainment/Finance/Food & Drink/Graphics & Design; 5 for Games/Health &
Fitness; 7 for Medical; 2 for Music/Navigation/Reference/Shopping/Social
Networking/Sports/Travel/Utilities/Weather). Codex is invited to add its
own to any category, or riff on these, per `conversations/PROTOCOL.md`.

Two ideas predate this categorized batch and aren't tied to a quota:
`pocket-warranty` (claude) and `favor-loop` (codex) — see
`ideas/README.md` Candidates table.

## Business

### Ledger Line
- one_liner: A margin calculator disguised as a musical staff, for freelancers who price gigs, projects, or line items by ear.
- pitch: Freelancers and small-shop owners constantly eyeball whether a quote is actually profitable, but spreadsheets bury the one number that matters (margin) under rows of tax and fee noise. Ledger Line renders each quote as a horizontal "staff" where cost, fees, and taxes are notes plotted below the line and profit rises above it, so profitability is something you see, not something you calculate. Built for solo contractors, photographers, and small studios who quote fast and need instant gut-check confidence before hitting send.
- design_hook: Every quote is a five-line staff; dragging a "note" (a cost or fee) up or down the staff live-redraws the profit bar above it and plays a soft pitch that rises with margin, so you can literally hear a deal get better or worse.
- monetization: Free for 3 quotes/month, then a one-time or annual unlock for unlimited quotes and CloudKit sync across devices.
- feasibility_one_dev: Single-view SwiftUI app with custom Canvas drawing for the staff, local calc logic, CloudKit for sync, StoreKit for the paywall — no backend, buildable solo in a few weeks.

### Overhead Weather
- one_liner: Turns your business's fixed costs into a daily "forecast" of how much you need to make before you're profitable.
- pitch: Small business owners (salons, food trucks, studios, consultants) know their monthly overhead but rarely feel it day to day, so slow days sneak up on them. Overhead Weather takes rent, subscriptions, payroll, and loan payments and spreads them into a daily "breakeven forecast" shown like a weather app, so every morning you see today's number you must clear before profit starts. It's aimed at owner-operators who want one glance, not a P&L.
- design_hook: The home screen looks like a weather app: a big animated condition icon (storm cloud to sunshine) reflects how far today's actual sales are from the breakeven line, with a "temperature" gauge that's really your margin percentage, and a 7-day forecast strip of upcoming breakeven pressure from known bills.
- monetization: Subscription (monthly/annual) since it's an ongoing daily-use tool tied to recurring bill tracking.
- feasibility_one_dev: Local-only data entry for recurring bills and daily revenue (manual or CSV import), SwiftUI with custom animated iconography, CloudKit sync, no live data feeds required — very buildable solo.

### Handoff
- one_liner: A visual "relay baton" app for tracking who owns the next action across a deal, project, or client relationship.
- pitch: Small teams and consultants juggling multiple deals lose deals not from lack of effort but from ambiguity over whose turn it is to act next — a problem generic task apps don't model well. Handoff represents every deal/project as a baton passed between people (you, client, vendor, teammate), and the entire app is organized around "who's holding the baton right now" rather than due dates or statuses. Built for consultants, agencies, and small B2B sales teams who live in email/calls but need one shared source of truth for momentum.
- design_hook: Each deal is a horizontal relay track with lane avatars; the baton icon visibly sits at whichever person's lane is "up," and tapping it to pass triggers a satisfying handoff animation with a haptic thunk, replacing status dropdowns entirely with a physical metaphor of momentum.
- monetization: Freemium: free for up to 5 active batons, subscription for unlimited plus shared CloudKit team boards.
- feasibility_one_dev: SwiftUI with CloudKit sharing (CKShare) for multi-person boards, custom lane/animation view, push notifications via APNs for "baton passed to you" — moderate scope but no custom server needed.

## Developer

### CrashLens
- one_liner: A crash log viewer that turns raw .ips and symbolicated crash reports into an interactive flame-map you can annotate and share.
- pitch: Solo and small-team iOS devs still read crash reports as scrolling monospace text in Xcode's Organizer or Console, losing context on recurring failure clusters across builds. CrashLens ingests dropped-in .ips/.crash files (or pulls from a local Xcode DerivedData/Console export) and groups them by thread, offset pattern, and binary image, giving a visual diff across app versions. Timely because more indies now ship without a backend crash service and just want a fast local triage tool.
- design_hook: Crashes render as a radial "blast map" — the crashing thread at the center, contributing threads arranged in concentric rings by proximity to the fault, color-coded by binary (app code vs system frameworks vs third-party); tapping a ring segment peels it outward into the raw stack trace like opening a folded map.
- monetization: One-time purchase or small subscription for multi-project history and PDF/Markdown export.
- feasibility_one_dev: Local file parsing + on-device symbolication is self-contained (no backend); a solid v1 is a few weeks for one developer.

### Podium
- one_liner: A StoreKit-native app for rehearsing and timing App Store product page (custom product pages / A-B test) copy and screenshots against real device frames before submission.
- pitch: Indie devs constantly guess how their App Store listing, screenshot set, and promotional text will actually look and read on-device, then discover cropping/truncation issues only after review. Podium lets you paste in your metadata and drag in screenshots, then previews them pixel-accurately across every current device size and locale-length variant, flagging truncation and contrast issues. Relevant now because Apple's custom product pages and in-app events multiply the number of listing variants a single developer must maintain by hand.
- design_hook: A horizontally-scrolling "shelf" of miniature device frames (like a physical App Store cap display) that all scrub together on one shared timeline scrubber, so dragging one slider replays your screenshot sequence simultaneously across iPhone SE through iPad Pro sizes and highlights where text clips in red.
- monetization: One-time purchase with an optional add-on pack for localized truncation-checking in additional languages.
- feasibility_one_dev: Pure on-device layout/rendering logic against known device specs, no network dependency; scoped well for a solo build.

### Longhand
- one_liner: A widget-first "commit journal" that turns your day's git commits (synced via CloudKit from a tiny CLI helper) into a handwritten-style changelog timeline on your phone.
- pitch: Solo developers lose track of what they actually did across scattered repos and rarely write changelogs or standups until forced to. Longhand's companion CLI (a single lightweight script, not a service) pushes commit metadata to a private CloudKit container, and the iOS app renders it as a personal, ambient timeline you glance at like a journal, prompting end-of-day summaries. Fits the current wave of "quantified work" and build-in-public habits among indie devs.
- design_hook: Commits render as ink-stroke ticks on a vertical timeline that thickens and darkens with commit size/frequency, styled like a hand-ruled notebook margin; a pinch gesture "unrolls" any day into a scroll-style detail view of individual commit messages in a monospace serif.
- monetization: Freemium — free single-repo timeline, subscription unlocks multi-repo/multi-device sync and weekly narrative summaries.
- feasibility_one_dev: CloudKit handles sync entirely (no custom backend); CLI is a thin git-hook wrapper, well within solo scope though it requires distributing/trusting a small companion script.

## Education

### FieldNote Botany
- one_liner: Point your camera at any plant on a walk and build a living, hand-drawn-style field journal of everything you've identified, with spaced botanical quizzes generated from your own discoveries.
- pitch: Nature-identification apps tell you a species name and move on; they don't teach retention or build a personal body of knowledge over a season. This is for hobbyist naturalists, homeschool families, and hikers who want identification to turn into actual learned botany, not a one-off lookup. Vision framework on-device plant/leaf recognition plus CloudKit sync lets a single developer ship this credibly without any ML infra of their own.
- design_hook: The journal renders each find as a hand-annotated specimen card (pressed-leaf paper texture, botanical illustration-style leader lines pointing to identifying features) rather than a photo-plus-caption list, and the quiz mode shuffles these cards into a tactile "sorting table" you drag species into family piles.
- monetization: Free for a limited number of journal entries per season, one-time or annual unlock for unlimited entries and quiz packs.
- feasibility_one_dev: Single dev feasible using Vision/Core ML plant classification (or on-device model + a bundled taxonomy dataset) and CloudKit for sync; no custom backend needed.

### Marginalia (reading recall)
- one_liner: An e-reader-companion app that turns your own book highlights and margin notes into a spaced-repetition course, so what you underline is what you actually remember.
- pitch: People highlight nonfiction constantly (Kindle, Apple Books, physical books photographed) and then never see that material again; the learning value evaporates the moment the book is closed. This targets lifelong learners, students, and nonfiction readers who want their reading time to compound into retained knowledge. Now is a good time because on-device text extraction (Vision OCR) and personal knowledge management are both trending, but nobody has connected "your highlights" directly to "your spaced review deck."
- design_hook: Highlights are laid out on a continuous vertical "scroll of marginalia" styled like an annotated manuscript page, with review cards physically torn from that scroll (a tear-line animation) when a note graduates from "reading" to "study deck," making the transition from passive highlight to active recall visually literal.
- monetization: Free for one active book/deck at a time; subscription unlocks unlimited books, OCR-from-photo import, and export.
- feasibility_one_dev: Single dev feasible: Vision OCR for photographed pages, direct file import for text-based highlights, on-device spaced-repetition scheduling, CloudKit for cross-device sync — no server required.
- note: name collides with Graphics & Design's `marginalia-layout` — different apps, disambiguated by slug.

### Cadence
- one_liner: Teaches rhythm, meter, and timing (musical or poetic) by having you physically tap and feel patterns rather than read notation, using haptics as the primary teaching medium.
- pitch: Rhythm is the single hardest thing to teach through text or standard notation apps, yet it's foundational to both music education and poetic scansion (iambic pentameter, etc.), and existing apps default to abstract notation that beginners can't feel. This is for music students, teachers, and poetry/spoken-word learners who need an embodied sense of timing rather than another theory quiz. It's newly viable because the Taptic Engine plus custom CHHapticPattern authoring can render nuanced, distinguishable rhythmic "textures" no web app can replicate.
- design_hook: Instead of notes-on-a-staff, patterns are shown as a horizontal "waveform of pulses" — thick and thin bars whose width encodes stress/duration — and the core interaction is tapping directly on that waveform in real time while matching haptic pulses play, turning learning into a call-and-response game rather than sheet-reading.
- monetization: Free starter pattern pack, one-time purchase to unlock the full curriculum (music meters + poetic scansion packs) as add-ons.
- feasibility_one_dev: Single dev feasible using Core Haptics and AVFoundation for audio-haptic sync, all pattern content authored locally; no backend, no ML needed.

## Entertainment

### Rolodex
- one_liner: A voicemail app that turns everyone in your contacts into a nightly radio show only you can hear.
- pitch: People hoard old voice memos, voicemails, and voice notes from friends and family but never revisit them, and the "record a voice memo" habit is otherwise low-payoff. Rolodex ingests voice recordings you import or record (birthday messages, rambly friend updates, old voicemail exports) and assembles them into a nightly "show" with generated bumpers, like a personal radio station of the people in your life. It's for people who are sentimental hoarders of voice media and want a passive, ambient way to revisit relationships rather than scrolling a flat list.
- design_hook: The whole app is styled as an analog radio dial/tuner — you physically turn a dial (rotation gesture) to move between "stations," each station being a person or era of your life, with a warm needle-and-frequency-numbers readout instead of a list UI.
- monetization: One-time unlock for unlimited stations plus a small subscription for AI-generated transition bumpers/monologues.
- feasibility_one_dev: On-device only — imports existing audio files/voicemails, uses on-device text-to-speech for bumpers, no server needed; CloudKit for cross-device sync of the library.

### Understudy
- one_liner: Point your camera at any movie or TV scene playing nearby and instantly get a karaoke-style overlay of the dialogue so you can perform it live, in sync, over the actors' voices.
- pitch: Movie-quote lip-syncing and scene reenactment are a huge organic trend (TikTok duets, POV audio memes) but there's no dedicated tool for it — people screen-record awkwardly instead. Understudy listens to audio playing in the room (or from an imported clip), matches it against a licensed/user-supplied subtitle or script track, and displays word-by-word karaoke timing overlaid on the live camera view so you can perform along in perfect sync, then exports the take. It's for the reenactment/duet/theater-kid TikTok crowd, and it fits now because on-device speech alignment is finally fast enough to feel instant.
- design_hook: The core interaction is a live camera viewfinder with a karaoke lyric bar that behaves like a physical film strip scrolling beneath the frame, word-highlighting bounces in time with detected audio, borrowing the visual language of a teleprompter crossed with subtitle karaoke rather than a typical captions overlay.
- monetization: Free with watermark/limited clips per week, subscription unlocks unlimited exports, no watermark, and script packs.
- feasibility_one_dev: Uses on-device Speech framework for alignment against a user-supplied or imported script/subtitle file (avoids licensing/infra of a scene database); ships as camera + AVFoundation export, no backend required.

### Afterglow
- one_liner: A shared "watch clock" widget that shows your friends' TV/movie watching in real time as glowing dots on a 24-hour ring, so you can see who's up for a sync-watch right now.
- pitch: Group chats constantly ask "is anyone watching anything tonight" but there's no lightweight, ambient way to broadcast "I'm free and want company" for watching something, distinct from heavyweight watch-party apps. Afterglow is a small circle of friends (5-15 people) who each get a dot on a shared 24-hour clock face widget; tapping a dot fires a "join me" ping, and the ring's glow position/color shows recency and mood (chill, hype, quiet). It's for close friend groups replacing "u up?" texts with an ambient presence signal, riding the current wave of ambient-status widgets.
- design_hook: The entire app is a single circular 24-hour clock face (like a watch face) with friends rendered as pulsing light dots at their current local-time position — the home screen widget IS the clock, and the interaction model is tapping/dragging a dot outward to "invite," rather than lists, feeds, or chat threads.
- monetization: Free for one circle of up to 5 friends, subscription for multiple circles/larger groups and custom clock themes.
- feasibility_one_dev: WidgetKit for the live clock-face widget, CloudKit for the small shared-presence sync between friends (no custom backend), StoreKit for subscription tiers.

## Finance

### Split-Second (bill splitting)
- one_liner: A widget-first bill-splitting ledger that lives on your Lock Screen and Home Screen widgets instead of inside an app you have to open.
- pitch: Groups of friends/roommates constantly owe each other small amounts and nobody wants to open yet another app to log it. Split-Second turns logging a shared expense into a Lock Screen widget tap and a Dynamic Island confirmation, with running balances always visible without unlocking the phone. Built for roommates, trip groups, and recurring dinner crews; now is right because widget interactivity and Live Activities finally make "at-a-glance finance" viable.
- design_hook: Balances are drawn as a horizontal tug-of-war rope between avatars (who owes whom slides a knot along a line) instead of a table of numbers, and settling up is a literal drag-the-knot-to-center gesture that triggers a satisfying snap animation and settlement receipt.
- monetization: Free for 2-person splits, subscription unlocks unlimited group members, recurring splits, and CSV/PDF export.
- feasibility_one_dev: SwiftUI + WidgetKit + CloudKit for shared group sync (no custom backend); the rope-physics interaction is the only nontrivial custom animation work.
- note: name collides with Sports' `splitsecond-pace` — different apps, disambiguated by slug.

### Sinking Fund Islands
- one_liner: An archipelago map where each saved-for goal (vacation, new laptop, emergency fund) is a literal island that physically grows and gets more detailed as you fund it.
- pitch: Generic "savings goal" progress bars are forgettable and people abandon them within weeks. This app is for visually-driven savers who want their sinking funds (irregular categories like "car repair," "gifts," "annual insurance") to feel like a world they're building rather than a percentage counter. Now is a good time because people increasingly manage money manually post-linked-account fatigue and privacy concerns, and want tactile, gamified tools that don't require bank access.
- design_hook: Each goal renders as a procedurally-generated isometric island on a shared ocean map; depositing money adds terrain, buildings, and trees to that island in real time (0% is a bare rock, 100% is a lush developed island), and zooming out shows your whole "financial archipelago" as a navigable world map.
- monetization: One-time unlock for unlimited islands/themes plus a small IAP pack for island cosmetic styles (seasonal, biome skins).
- feasibility_one_dev: Fully local/manual entry (no bank linking) using SwiftUI + SceneKit or RealityKit for the isometric islands; procedural generation logic is bounded and reusable across islands, CloudKit syncs across devices.

### Receipt Fossil
- one_liner: A camera-first app that turns photographed receipts into a scrollable "strata" timeline of your spending, like sediment layers building up over time.
- pitch: People who don't want automated bank tracking (freelancers, cash-heavy spenders, privacy-conscious users, or people in regions with weak Open Banking support) still want a record of purchases without manual typing. Receipt Fossil uses on-device Vision text recognition to scan receipts and auto-extract merchant/amount/date, then stacks them chronologically as visual "rock layers" you can scroll down through like digging through time. Now is a good moment because on-device VisionKit OCR is accurate enough to skip cloud OCR services entirely, keeping it privacy-first and infra-light.
- design_hook: The main view is a vertical geological cross-section: each week compresses into a colored sediment band (color/texture derived from spending category mix), today's spending sits as loose "topsoil" at the top, and tapping any layer cracks it open to reveal the individual receipts fossilized inside it.
- monetization: Free for the last 30 days of layers; subscription unlocks unlimited history, category rules, and export to CSV/PDF.
- feasibility_one_dev: On-device VisionKit/Vision framework for OCR (no server), Core Data for storage, custom SwiftUI Canvas drawing for the sediment-layer visualization is the main design investment but has no backend dependency.

## Food & Drink

### Steeped
- one_liner: A tea and coffee brewing companion that turns each steep into a visual timeline you tune by taste, not by generic timers.
- pitch: Serious tea/coffee drinkers (gongfu tea, pour-over, cold brew) constantly re-guess steep times, water temp, and ratios across many small brews in one session, and generic kitchen timers don't capture iterative multi-infusion sessions. This is for the enthusiast who resteeps the same leaves 5-8 times and wants to remember what worked last time. Now is a good moment because specialty tea/coffee subscriptions and home gear (gooseneck kettles, gaiwans) have surged, but the software side is still just stopwatches.
- design_hook: The core screen is a radial "steep ring" that fills like an infusion darkening in a cup — each concentric ring represents one resteep of the same leaves, colored by your own after-the-fact taste rating (a swipe-up/down gesture on the ring), so a session becomes a readable gradient bloom rather than a list of timer entries.
- monetization: One-time unlock or small annual subscription for unlimited saved brew profiles and iCloud sync via CloudKit.
- feasibility_one_dev: Local timers/haptics plus CloudKit sync only, no backend — well within solo scope.

### Pantry Ghost
- one_liner: A camera-first pantry tracker that visualizes what's quietly expiring as fading photographic "ghosts" on a shelf.
- pitch: People forget what's in the back of the fridge/pantry until it spoils, and manual inventory apps die from data-entry fatigue. This targets home cooks trying to cut food waste without adopting a full grocery-management habit. Vision framework's on-device object/text recognition now makes snap-to-log fast enough to actually stick, which wasn't true a few years ago.
- design_hook: You photograph a shelf; each item becomes a small cutout "ghost" icon pinned to a shelf-diagram of your actual pantry, and as its estimated expiry approaches the icon desaturates and grows translucent day by day — so the whole pantry view itself communicates urgency, no list, no dates required.
- monetization: Free with limited shelves; subscription unlocks unlimited shelves and multi-pantry (fridge/freezer/pantry) tracking.
- feasibility_one_dev: On-device Vision for text/object detection, local storage, no server-side ML — solo-buildable, main risk is recognition accuracy tuning.

### Proof (tasting journal)
- one_liner: A cocktail-and-spirits tasting journal that plots every dram or pour on a shared flavor-space map instead of a star rating.
- pitch: Whiskey, wine, and cocktail enthusiasts want to track and compare tastings, but star ratings flatten nuance and existing apps feel like spreadsheets. This is for hobbyist collectors and home bartenders who want to see how a new bottle relates to ones they already love. Now is timely given the boom in home bartending and whiskey/agave collecting communities that already obsess over flavor notes.
- design_hook: Instead of lists, every tasting drops a labeled dot onto a 2D flavor map (axes like smoky-to-fruity, light-to-bold) that you position yourself with a drag gesture, and your whole collection becomes a personal constellation you can pan/zoom, cluster, and compare pours against.
- monetization: One-time purchase for the app, with an optional subscription for unlimited flavor-map collections and StoreKit-driven "collection packs" (e.g., preset maps for whiskey vs. mezcal vs. wine).
- feasibility_one_dev: Pure on-device data viz and CoreData/CloudKit storage, no external APIs needed — very solo-friendly.

## Graphics & Design

### PixelWitness
- one_liner: A screenshot-forensics editor that visually diffs, annotates, and redacts image evidence for support tickets, bug reports, and disputes.
- pitch: Designers, QA engineers, and support agents constantly send screenshots back and forth trying to describe "what changed" or "what's wrong" in words. PixelWitness lets you drop two screenshots (or a before/after pair) and get an automatic pixel-diff overlay you can then annotate, mask, and export as a single annotated artifact. It's for anyone who files bug reports, disputes charges, or documents UI regressions and is tired of arrows-in-Preview.
- design_hook: The signature interaction is a physical "slide-to-reveal" wipe divider between the two images (like a film-negative loupe) instead of a static side-by-side or ghosted overlay, with diff "hotspots" rendered as glowing pulse-rings sized by how much that region changed.
- monetization: Free with 3 exports/month, one-time unlock or subscription for unlimited exports and batch/CloudKit sync across devices.
- feasibility_one_dev: Core diffing is Core Image/Accelerate pixel math plus PencilKit-style annotation layer — no server, all on-device, very buildable solo.

### Swatchbound
- one_liner: A camera-first color palette extractor that turns real-world scenes into licensed-safe, harmony-corrected design palettes.
- pitch: Designers constantly want to pull a palette from a photo (a sunset, a fabric, a street sign) but existing tools just dump the 5 most common pixels, which often clash. Swatchbound uses the camera live-preview to extract colors in real time and instantly reflows them onto a proper harmony wheel (complementary/triadic/analogous), so what you point the camera at becomes an immediately usable, exportable palette. It's aimed at indie designers, brand/social content creators, and needlepoint/paint-matching hobbyists.
- design_hook: Live camera view has a floating "harmony wheel" HUD overlaid on the viewfinder that rotates and snaps as detected colors shift, so choosing a palette feels like tuning a dial rather than picking swatches from a grid.
- monetization: Free basic extraction; subscription unlocks export formats (ASE, Procreate .swatches, CSS variables, Figma tokens) and unlimited saved palettes with CloudKit sync.
- feasibility_one_dev: Uses Vision/Core Image for color clustering, all on-device with no ML training needed — a focused, shippable solo scope.

### Marginalia (page layout)
- one_liner: A typeface-pairing and print-margin visualizer that lets you design book/zine layouts by dragging physical page proportions, not pixel values.
- pitch: Self-publishers, zine-makers, and indie authors laying out books in Word or Canva have no easy way to preview classical page-margin systems (Van de Graaf canon, golden section, rule of ninths) or see how a serif/sans pairing actually reads across a full spread before exporting to print. Marginalia is a focused layout-proofing tool: pick a trim size, drag a canon-based margin diagram directly on the page, and preview real paragraph text flowing into it with your chosen type pairing. It targets the current wave of self-publishing/zine creators who care about print craft but don't own InDesign.
- design_hook: The core UI is a diagrammatic page-construction overlay (the geometric canon lines used by historic book designers) rendered as draggable construction lines directly on the page mockup, rather than numeric margin input fields — you shape the page like a draftsman, not a form-filler.
- monetization: Free single trim-size/template; one-time purchase unlocks all canons, custom trim sizes, and PDF spread export.
- feasibility_one_dev: Pure geometry, typography rendering (Core Text) and PDF export — no ML, no backend, well-scoped for one developer.
- note: name collides with Education's `marginalia-reading` — different apps, disambiguated by slug.

## Games

### Ricochet Vault
- one_liner: A one-finger safecracking game where you aim a single bouncing ball through mirrored chambers to hit every target before it runs out of momentum.
- pitch: You draw one trajectory line per level; the ball launches and ricochets off angled walls, and you must find the single path that clips every switch/gem in the room before friction stops it. Aimed at puzzle fans who like Peggle-style physics but want a slower, solvable-not-random puzzle. Physics puzzlers with deliberate (non-random) solutions are underrepresented on mobile right now.
- design_hook: Entire game is controlled with a single drag-and-release "slingshot" gesture per level — no joystick, no taps mid-run — and the visual language is engraved brass/glass vault interiors lit only by the glowing trail of the ball's path.
- monetization: Paid unlock ($3.99) for level packs after a free 20-level demo, no ads.
- feasibility_one_dev: 2D physics via SpriteKit, procedural-friendly level format, small art set (vault tilesets) — very doable solo in 3-4 months.

### Marrow
- one_liner: A turn-based skeleton-assembly strategy game where you build your fighter's body from looted bones before every battle.
- pitch: Before each short combat encounter you drag interchangeable bone pieces (arms, ribs, tails, skulls) onto a humanoid rig; each bone slot changes your attack options and stat trade-offs, then you fight a 3-5 turn tactical battle. Aimed at strategy-minded players who like deckbuilders but want a physical, spatial twist on "building your kit." The hook is inventory-as-anatomy: min-maxing literally means designing a monster.
- design_hook: The build screen renders your creature as an x-ray-style skeletal diagram that visibly reconfigures as you drag bones on/off, and combat animations are built from that same skeletal rig so a stronger arm literally swings harder on screen.
- monetization: Free with a modest one-time unlock for the full campaign (~$4.99), cosmetic bone skins as IAP.
- feasibility_one_dev: Turn-based combat logic + a rigged 2D skeletal animation system (Spine or custom) is the biggest lift; scoped to ~40 bones and 15 encounters it's a realistic 4-6 month solo build.

### Tideline
- one_liner: A tilt-free tidal puzzle game where you redraw the coastline each turn to guide a paper boat home before the tide swallows it.
- pitch: Each level is an origami-paper-textured bay; you sketch short ink strokes on the water to raise sandbars and redirect currents, nudging a drifting paper boat toward a lighthouse over a fixed number of tide cycles. Meant for calm, low-pressure puzzle players (Alto's Odyssey audience) who want a mechanic they haven't seen: terrain-drawing instead of terrain-navigating. Fits the current wave of interest in "cozy strategy" without being a clone of any specific title.
- design_hook: Ink-stroke drawing is the entire control scheme — no buttons — and the whole game is rendered in a wet-on-wet watercolor shader so every ink stroke actually blooms and spreads realistically across the paper-textured water.
- monetization: Paid app ($2.99), no IAP, no ads — matches the calm/premium positioning.
- feasibility_one_dev: Core is 2D fluid-ish simulation (can be faked with flow-field + shader) plus stroke input; scope to ~50 hand-tuned levels, achievable solo in 4-5 months with a custom shader as the main risk.

### Static Choir
- one_liner: A radio-tuning rhythm game where you turn a physical dial to align pitch-shifting enemy "signals" instead of tapping notes.
- pitch: Enemies are broadcast waveforms scrolling toward you; you rotate an on-screen tuning dial to match their pitch/frequency in real time, and successful tuning "silences" them in rhythm with the track. Built for rhythm-game fans bored of lane-tappers, who want a continuous analog control instead of discrete note hits. The dial control turns rhythm gameplay into a tracking/precision skill rather than a memorization skill.
